The Collective Position Paper is out

Written and voted by the participants of the Erasmus+ Future Forum, the Paper sets out four demands for the 2028–2034 programme. From today, organisations and professionals can sign it.

15 July 2026·2 min read

Six weeks after the Forum in Leipzig, the Collective Position Paper on the future of Erasmus+ (2028–2034) is published. It turns four days of work by networks, associations, trainers and public authorities into a short set of demands for the European Parliament, the European Commission, the Council and the National Agencies.

The Paper was written collaboratively during the Forum and voted by its participants. It does not speak for one organisation: it speaks for the people who deliver Erasmus+ on the ground, and it is now open for others to join.

Read the full Paper The Paper in brief

The four demands

01

A budget that matches the ambition

At least €60 billion for 2028–2034, indexed for inflation across the whole period, with a fairer distribution, higher recognition for Erasmus+ Youth and earmarks for each sector.

02

Skills for everyone, not a pipeline for the few

Labour-market skills as a horizontal priority that complements, never overrides, inclusion, citizenship and intercultural learning, with at least 25% of the Erasmus+ Youth budget ring-fenced for inclusion, participation and intercultural dialogue.

03

A standalone European Solidarity Corps

The Corps kept as its own programme, with its own objectives, governance and visibility, working with Erasmus+ through shared tools and a single entry point rather than a merger.

04

From participation to recognition

A stronger Youthpass, connected to Europass and recognised in education and on the labour market, within a flexible recognition system for non-formal learning.

The new Erasmus+ must build a Union of Skills for everyone, not a pipeline for the few: employability matters, but so do solidarity, intercultural understanding and active citizenship.

Why now

The Commission’s proposal for 2028–2034 merges Erasmus+ with the European Solidarity Corps and places the programme under the Union of Skills. The Council is already working on its position, and the European Parliament’s Culture and Education Committee is preparing its report, with a vote expected after the summer. The next months decide the shape of the programme for seven years. The full argument for each demand, with the evidence behind it, is on the Position Paper page.

Sign the Paper

The Paper is open for signature by organisations and networks and by professionals signing in their own name: youth workers, trainers, educators, and anyone working with Erasmus+. Signing takes two minutes. Signatory organisations are listed on the signatories page.

Sign the Paper

Over the coming weeks, we will share the Paper with Members of the European Parliament, the Commission and national decision-makers. The more organisations stand behind it, the harder it is to ignore.
